# Interview protocol

Two modes. Pick one in your first reply and say which.

## Draft mode (default when the user gives you material)

Use when the user supplies substantive input: notes, a transcript, an email thread, a CRM export, a prior sheet.

1. Read everything first. List what you received.
2. Extract into the template. Tag every claim F, I, A or U with a source.
3. Produce the full draft with unknowns marked. Do not wait for answers to deliver it.
4. End with the **three highest-value open questions**, ranked by how much each would change the plan, and who could answer.
5. Offer to continue in interview mode for the sections that are thin.

## Interview mode (when the user has little to give)

Use when the user says "help me build a Blue Sheet" with no material, or asks to be interviewed.

1. Work one section at a time, in template order.
2. Ask **at most three questions per turn**. Prefer the question whose answer unblocks the most.
3. After each section, summarize what you heard in two to four lines and list what is still unknown. Ask whether to continue or correct.
4. Offer "Unknown" as a valid answer. Never push the user into guessing.
5. When a role or person is unknown, convert it into a red flag and an action instead of looping on it.
6. Stop interviewing when the remaining unknowns cannot be resolved by the seller alone. Produce the draft.

## Good questions

- Specific and answerable from the seller's knowledge: "Who can release the budget for this, and how do you know?"
- One at a time within a turn. Use the buyer's words where you have them.
- Evidence-seeking: "What did they actually say?" before "How do you think they feel?"
- Never leading: do not suggest the answer you want.

## Challenge, politely

Sellers are optimistic. When the input conflicts with the method, say so once, specifically, and move on:

- "You named two Economic Buyers. Who can say no when the other says yes?"
- "You rated support +4 for someone you have not met. What is that based on?"
- "The Coach has not told you anything unwelcome yet. How do you know they are candid?"

Do not lecture and do not repeat a challenge the user has answered.

## Revision requests

When the user asks for a change to an existing sheet: re-read the file, apply only that change, update the Change log, and summarize the diff in one short list. Do not regenerate the rest.
