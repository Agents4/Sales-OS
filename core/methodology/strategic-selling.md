# Strategic Selling and the Blue Sheet

Tags: **[C]** canonical, **[V]** convention, **[X]** Sales-OS extension.

## Premise [C]

Plan each important opportunity around the *people* who influence the decision. The Blue Sheet is a one-page working map of the deal. If you cannot fill it in honestly, you do not yet understand the deal, and the blanks tell you what to learn next.

## Step 1. Single Sales Objective (SSO) [C]

One sentence stating exactly what you want from this opportunity and by when. It names the account, the scope, the value or quantity, and a date.

- Good: "Signed 3-year agreement with Halcyon Logistics for the Meridian Route Platform across 4 terminals, 640K USD annual recurring revenue, signature by 2026-12-18."
- Bad: "Win Halcyon."
- **The colleague test [V]:** could a colleague read it and say on the date whether you hit it or missed it?
- One SSO per sheet. If you need "and" between two outcomes, you have two opportunities and two sheets.
- An SSO is what *you* want. It is not the buyer's goal. The buyer's goal is their Concept (see `conceptual-selling.md`).

## Step 2. Buying Influences [C]

Every complex sale has four kinds of buying influence. Identify the actual people. Roles are not titles: a junior person can be the Coach and a CEO can be a User.

| Influence | What they do | Rule of thumb |
|---|---|---|
| **Economic Buyer** | Gives final approval, can release discretionary funds | **Exactly one.** Can say yes when everyone else says no, and no when everyone else says yes. Often hard to reach. Depends on deal size and risk, not title. |
| **User Buyer** | Lives with the result day to day; judges the impact on their own job | Many. They cannot approve, but their judgment shapes adoption and can quietly sink the deal or the renewal. |
| **Technical Buyer** | Screens against specifications: security, integration, legal, procurement, finance review | Can say no, cannot say yes. Treat as a gate, not a decision-maker. |
| **Coach** | Guides you through their organization | Must pass three tests below. Not the same as a friendly contact or a Champion who only sells for you. |

**The three Coach tests [C, as commonly taught]:** (1) credibility with the other buying influences in *this* account, (2) wants you to win this deal (a Win of their own is tied to it), (3) gives you information you could not get elsewhere, including unwelcome news. A person who is friendly but fails the tests is a contact, not a Coach. Coaches are developed, not found: you earn one by helping them win.

One person can hold more than one role. One role can have several people, except Economic Buyer.

**Degree of influence [C]:** how much weight this person carries in *this* decision, High, Medium or Low, regardless of title.

## Step 3. Response Modes [C]

Each person is in one of four modes about the issue your solution addresses. Mode is per person and per issue, and it changes. Re-assess it after every interaction.

| Mode | The person sees | Ready to change? | What to do | Pitfall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Growth** | A gap between where they are and where they could be; an opportunity | Yes | Show how you help them reach it; align to their vision | Treating growth enthusiasm as a budget |
| **Trouble** | Something is wrong now; pain | Yes | Diagnose, quantify, show a fast path to relief | Selling a quick fix that makes the next problem |
| **Even Keel** | No gap; satisfied | No | Do not sell to them yet. Help them see what they are not seeing (risk, cost of inaction, peers' results), or find a Growth or Trouble buyer or a later window | Reading politeness as support |
| **Overconfident** | Things are better than they are | No | Give an honest, evidence-based reality check, often via a trusted third party or their own data; do not argue | Taking their confidence at face value, or confronting them |

The principle [C]: people in Growth or Trouble will change and will buy; people in Even Keel or Overconfident will not until their perception changes. An Economic Buyer in Even Keel or Overconfident is a timing problem, not a supporter: either change their mode or change the forecast.

## Step 4. Win-Results [C]

For each buying influence define two things:

- **Result:** a measurable business outcome the purchase produces for the organization or their area.
- **Win:** the personal payoff the individual collects: for example recognition, advancement, risk reduction, control, ease, peace of mind, growth.

People buy on Win-Results together. A Win without a Result gives you a friendly contact and a weak renewal. A Result without a Win gives you a stalled deal and a quietly disengaging champion. Neither is a loss. Both is a close and a reference. [V]

- If you cannot write the personal Win, you have not yet had a real conversation with that person.
- **Ethics [X]:** sell a Win the buyer will actually receive. Selling a Win you know they will not get is manipulation, and it destroys the account later.
- **Privacy [X]:** record work-relevant motives only. Never record health, family, finances beyond the business role, politics, religion or other private-life details.
- To uncover a Win, ask attitude questions (see `conceptual-selling.md`): what success would mean for them, what they are worried about, what it would do for their standing.

## Step 5. Red Flags [C]

A red flag is anything missing, unknown, uncertain or changing that could keep you from winning. The classic triggers:

1. Missing information about a buying influence (role, mode, Win-Result).
2. Uncertainty about information you do have.
3. A buying influence you have not contacted.
4. Someone new to their job (often 60-90 days) whose priorities are not yet settled.
5. A reorganization in the buying account, which invalidates the whole map at once.

Sales-OS adds [X]: no identified Economic Buyer; one Coach only; single-threaded contact; a mode of Even Keel or Overconfident on the Economic Buyer; a security or legal gate whose lead time exceeds the runway; a strength cited to offset a flag on a different influence.

**Rule [C]:** a strength on one side of the deal does not cancel a red flag on the other. Every red flag must be eliminated or offset by a named action with an owner and a date.

## Step 6. Strengths and Competition [C]

- **Strengths:** things working for you, tied to an influence (a relationship, a proven result, alignment to a criterion). Use them in the plan.
- **Competition:** every alternative, including the **status quo (do nothing)** and an internal build. For each, how the buying influences perceive it, its strengths and weaknesses, and your counter. The status quo is usually the strongest competitor.
- **Position [V]:** your honest read against the field: front-runner, in the mix, behind, unknown. A position needs a reason with evidence.

## Step 7. Action Plan [C]

Turn the analysis into dated actions with owners.

- Each action targets a red flag or builds on a strength. Actions with no owner or date are wishes.
- **Best Action Commitment and Minimum Acceptable Action [C]:** for the next interaction, name the most the buyer is realistically ready to do, and the least that still advances the sale. If you cannot get the minimum, reassess the deal. (These come from Conceptual Selling; see that file.)
- Sequence access from problem owners upward to the Economic Buyer. Use the Coach for access and intelligence, not for pricing or strategy decisions.

## Re-walk, do not fill once [C]

The Blue Sheet is a living document. Update it after every meaningful interaction and re-walk the whole sheet on a fixed rhythm: weekly for Best Few opportunities. Date your changes so the sheet does not go stale. Trigger an immediate re-walk on: a reorganization, a new executive, a competitor move, a missed commitment, or a surprise from the Coach.

## Scales and fields: what is canonical and what is not

- **Canonical [C]:** SSO, the four buying influences, degree of influence, response modes, Win-Results, red flags, strengths, competition, action plan, the re-walk principle.
- **Convention [V]:** the -5 to +5 *Support* rating, Position labels, the fit scores for the Ideal Customer Profile, severity labels. Published sources and vendors' templates differ on these. Sales-OS ships sensible defaults (documented in the templates); a client with official Miller Heiman training should be able to substitute its own scales.
- **Extensions [X]:** evidence tagging (F, I, A, U), source and confidence columns, the unknowns register, the decision-process table, the change log, lint rules.
