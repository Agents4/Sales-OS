# The Miller Heiman system at a glance

This knowledge base is a practitioner synthesis of the Miller Heiman sales methodology, written for AI agents and sellers who plan complex B2B deals. It is not an official publication of Korn Ferry or Miller Heiman Group, and it does not reproduce their copyrighted workbooks. Each element is tagged so you always know what is canonical and what is our convention (see `fidelity-ledger.md`):

- **[C] Canonical.** Appears in the published methodology or official training terminology.
- **[V] Convention.** Widely used practitioner practice (scales, thresholds, cadences). Reasonable defaults, tune them.
- **[X] Sales-OS extension.** Added by this system because it makes the method work better with AI assistance.

Strategic Selling, Conceptual Selling, Large Account Management Process (LAMP) and the Blue, Green and Gold Sheets are the property of their owner (Miller Heiman Group, part of Korn Ferry). If you deploy this for a client, confirm licensing and keep official materials separate from this synthesis.

## One idea underneath everything

A company cannot buy anything. People buy. Complex sales are won by understanding each person who influences the decision: what role they play, how ready they are to change, what the organization needs from the decision (the **Result**), and what they personally gain (the **Win**). Everything in the three tools below is a disciplined way to find that out and act on it.

## Three planning altitudes [C]

| Altitude | Methodology | Tool | Scope | The question it answers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Account | Large Account Management Process (LAMP) | Gold Sheet | One strategic account, or a Field of Play inside it, over 12-36 months | Where should this relationship go, and what do we invest in to get there? |
| Opportunity | Strategic Selling | Blue Sheet | One opportunity with one Single Sales Objective | How do we win this deal? |
| Conversation | Conceptual Selling | Green Sheet | One call or meeting | How do we run this conversation so it moves the sale? |

They nest. A Gold Sheet spawns Single Sales Objectives, each of which gets a Blue Sheet, each of which needs a Green Sheet before every important conversation. After the conversation, learning flows back up: Green to Blue to Gold.

```
Gold Sheet  (account, quarterly review)
   └─ Blue Sheet  (opportunity, weekly review)
         └─ Green Sheet  (meeting, before and after every call)
```

## Origins and ownership [C]

Robert B. Miller and Stephen E. Heiman, with Tad Tuleja, published Strategic Selling (first edition 1985), Conceptual Selling (1987) and Successful Large Account Management (1991), each later revised as "The New ...". The firm became Miller Heiman Group and now operates under Korn Ferry. Korn Ferry's CRM-enablement product for the methodology is called Korn Ferry Sell.

## The operating rhythm [X]

Sales-OS turns the method into a loop an agent can run reliably:

1. **Plan.** Build or refresh the right sheet (`mh-blue-sheet`, `mh-green-sheet`, `mh-gold-sheet`).
2. **Execute.** The seller runs the conversation.
3. **Debrief.** Within 24 hours, convert notes or the transcript into evidence and sheet updates (`mh-call-debrief`).
4. **Review.** Re-walk the sheet weekly for Best Few opportunities (`mh-pipeline-review`, `mh-deal-review`), quarterly for accounts.
5. **Learn.** After wins and losses, compare the sheet to the outcome and correct the rules (`mh-win-loss-review`, `sos-ratchet`).

## When to use the method, and when not to [X]

Use it for complex, high-consideration B2B sales: several people influence the decision, the cycle is long, the value is high enough to justify planning time. Do not impose a full Blue Sheet on a small transactional deal; a short qualification check is enough (see `funnel-and-pipeline.md`). A method applied everywhere becomes paperwork and loses credibility with sellers.
