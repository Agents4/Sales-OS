# LAMP and the Gold Sheet

Tags: **[C]** canonical (official LAMP terminology), **[V]** convention, **[X]** Sales-OS extension.

## Premise [C]

LAMP plans the relationship with a strategic account. "Large" means accounts you cannot afford to lose or where the future value is highest, not simply the biggest by revenue. Where Strategic Selling asks "how do we win this deal", LAMP asks "where should this relationship go, and what do we invest in, and stop investing in, to get there?"

The Gold Sheet (also called the Field of Play Analysis and Strategy) is a living document in three columns read left to right: **Situational Appraisal** (where we stand), **Strategy** (where we should go), **Action Plan** (how we get there).

## The four phases [C]

1. **Data Gathering.** Select the Strategic Account, choose the Field of Play, identify the Key Players, and collect information on trends, KPIs, challenges and revenue history.
2. **Situation Appraisal.** Map Key Players to Strategic Player roles, build a cross-functional internal account team, and identify trends and opportunities, your Strategic Strengths and your Critical Vulnerability.
3. **Strategy Planning.** Assess your position on the Buy-Sell Hierarchy, write the Charter Statement, set Relationship Goals, make Focus and Stop investment decisions, and set Revenue Targets.
4. **Action Planning.** Define Single Sales Objectives and Investment Programs, eliminate red flags, obtain leadership sign-off, and schedule reviews (60 to 90 days).

## Terminology [C]

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Strategic Account** | An account worth a LAMP plan. Selection is a decision, not an entitlement (see account selection below). |
| **Field of Play** | The segment of a large account that becomes the focus of your strategy: it acts independently and you can contribute significantly. Large accounts often have several. |
| **Key Players / Strategic Players** | People within the Field of Play, assigned one of four roles below. |
| **Sponsor** | A Strategic Player who promotes your relationship within their organization and counters the competition's efforts. At least one is essential. |
| **Strategic Coach** | A Strategic Player who helps you understand the Field of Play and how it makes decisions. |
| **Anti-Sponsor** | A Strategic Player who opposes your efforts and works to negate you or your Sponsors. |
| **Additional Key Player** | Anyone else with influence over the relationship. |
| **Strategic Strengths** | What you bring that the Field of Play values and competitors cannot easily match. |
| **Critical Vulnerability** | A significant weakness that, if left unaddressed, would cripple your strategic position in the Field of Play. Internal (missing expertise, capacity) or external (regulation, an acquisition). |
| **Buy-Sell Hierarchy** | A way to categorize the current and desired relationship from the buyer's and seller's perspectives (five levels, below). |
| **Charter Statement** | Where the selling organization and the Field of Play mutually agree the relationship should go: the Field of Play, the benefit to its business, how you add value, the benefit you receive. |
| **Relationship Goal** | How you want a Strategic Player to perceive you. Links a player to an opportunity and a Strategic Strength. |
| **Focus Investments** | Organizational resources you apply to help the Field of Play act on its opportunities. Four categories: relationship development, geographic representation, research and development, operational infrastructure. |
| **Stop Investments** | Decisions to discontinue an investment that no longer earns a worthwhile return, freeing resources. |
| **Revenue Targets** | Revenue anticipated from the Field of Play in a defined period, depending on actions taken. A planning hypothesis until milestones are confirmed. |
| **Investment Program** | A set of actions that deliver on a Focus investment. |

**Strategic Players are not the Blue Sheet roles.** A Blue Sheet maps Economic, User and Technical Buyers and a Coach for *one* decision. A Gold Sheet maps Sponsors, Strategic Coaches, Anti-Sponsors and Additional Key Players for the *relationship*. The same person can appear in both with different roles.

## The Buy-Sell Hierarchy [C]

| Level | The customer sees you as |
|---|---|
| 1 | A commodity vendor, competing mainly on price |
| 2 | Differentiated by features |
| 3 | Valued for service and support |
| 4 | Helping senior leaders achieve measurable business objectives |
| 5 | A trusted advisor to the senior executive team, an invaluable long-term asset |

Assess it from both views: where the customer places you and where you place yourself. The customer decides the level, not you. Gaps between the two views are findings. Choose a desired level and make Focus investments that move you there. Moving up usually requires relationships at a higher level and proof of business impact.

## Account selection [V]

Not every account deserves a Gold Sheet. A scoring rubric helps you choose. Sales-OS uses seven factors, each scored 1-5: current value, future potential, strategic fit, mutual value, relationship potential, delivery fit, resource return. Use it to rank candidates; it is a tool for judgment, not a formula.

## What a good Gold Sheet shows [X]

- Evidence behind every statement: expected versus achieved outcomes, customer priorities, objections, commitments, upcoming decisions.
- A relationship map built from direct interaction with multiple sources, not intuition. An org chart is not a relationship map.
- A named Critical Vulnerability and a plan to address it.
- A Charter Statement that is measurable and time-bound.
- At least one Stop investment. A plan with only things to do is a task list.
- Single Sales Objectives that link to Blue Sheets, and Green Sheets for the conversations that move them.

## Review rhythm [V]

- **Weekly:** open actions, customer commitments, immediate risks, upcoming meetings.
- **Monthly:** stakeholder changes, opportunity evidence, coverage, initiative progress.
- **Quarterly (60-90 days, per the official guidance):** charter, position, Focus and Stop decisions, outcomes, revenue targets.
- **Triggers:** executive change, acquisition or reorganization, Sponsor departure, competitive entry, new initiatives, major delivery incident.

## Fidelity note

The four phases, terminology, Buy-Sell Hierarchy and three-column Gold Sheet follow official training terminology. The account selection rubric, review cadence by week and month, and the evidence standards are practitioner conventions or Sales-OS extensions. Earlier versions of Sales-OS used Blue Sheet roles for the Gold Sheet and an invented four-step position ladder (Vendor, Credible Source, Problem Solver, Strategic Partner). Those have been replaced by the official terms above.
