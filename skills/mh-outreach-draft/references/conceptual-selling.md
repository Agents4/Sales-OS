# Conceptual Selling and the Green Sheet

Tags: **[C]** canonical, **[V]** convention, **[X]** Sales-OS extension.

## Premise [C]

Customers do not buy products. They buy the **Concept**: their own mental picture of what the world looks like once the problem is solved. Two people in the same account usually hold different Concepts. The seller's job in a conversation is to discover the buyer's Concept, test it, and then show how the solution fits it. Sell to the Concept, not to the product.

## The call has three parts [C]

1. **Getting information.** Diagnose the Concept with questions. This is most of the call.
2. **Giving information.** Present only the parts of your solution that match the Concept you have diagnosed. Do not give information before you have earned it by getting information.
3. **Getting commitment.** Ask for a specific next step that moves the sale.

Practitioners often wrap these with an opening that sets purpose and agenda, and a close that verifies what was agreed in writing [V]. Sales-OS treats "set" and "verify" as part of the Green Sheet.

## Question types [C]

The Green Sheet plans the questions in advance. Five types, each with a job:

| Type | Job | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Confirmation** | Verify what you believe is true so the conversation starts from shared ground; catches stale assumptions | "Last time you said the committee meets on 12 November. Is that still the date?" |
| **New information** | Learn what you do not know: the Concept, criteria, process, who else matters | "When your COO looks at the savings model, what would she want to see on the first page?" |
| **Attitude** | Surface feelings, priorities, politics and personal stakes; reveals mode and Win | "How do you feel about bringing her in before the committee meets?" |
| **Commitment** | Test readiness and earn small agreements on the way to the Best Action Commitment | "If we show her the Memphis number in 30 minutes, would you set that up before the 30th?" |
| **Basic issue** | Reach the underlying business issue beneath the presenting problem and the consequence of inaction | "If nothing changes this year, what does that cost you in front of the board?" |

Craft rules [V]: ask one question at a time; follow a new-information question with a confirmation or attitude question rather than firing a list; use the buyer's words; never ask a question whose answer you could have found in advance.

## The Realistic Objective: Best Action Commitment and Minimum Acceptable Action [C]

Before the call, write two things:

- **Best Action Commitment (BAC):** the most the buyer is realistically ready to do next. It must be specific and dated and must advance the sale even if the order is not ready (an introduction to the Economic Buyer, a security review kickoff, a data share). It is not "get a decision".
- **Minimum Acceptable Action (MAA):** the least that still moves the deal. If you cannot get this, the call tells you something about the deal: reassess.

If you cannot state a valid business reason *from the buyer's side* for agreeing to the BAC, you are asking for a favor, not a commitment.

## The Green Sheet [C]

The Green Sheet plans one conversation and is walked before and after it. Sales-OS template sections:

1. Realistic Objective (BAC, MAA, valid reason from their side)
2. Buyer's Concept (hypothesis, in their words, with confidence)
3. Known, assumed, unknown [X]
4. Questions by type
5. Giving information: parts of the Concept and what you will show, in order
6. Obstacles and responses [X]
7. Verify and recap
8. Post-call debrief, which feeds the Blue Sheet

## Win-Results in the conversation [C]

Conceptual Selling has you diagnose Result and Win for each buyer on each call. The Green Sheet is the conversation-level view and the Blue Sheet is the opportunity-level view; the two are used together. Anything learned in a call about a person's mode, Win or Concept goes back into the Blue Sheet within a day.

## Joint venture framing [V]

Practitioners describe the ideal close as a co-authored decision: the buyer's Concept is matched, both sides commit to actions, and the next step feels like theirs rather than something they were maneuvered into. In Sales-OS this shows up as the "what we will do / what they will do" line of the verification step.

## After the call: the debrief [X]

Within 24 hours:

1. Record what was learned as dated facts with source.
2. Write the Concept in the buyer's own words.
3. Compare the outcome to BAC and MAA.
4. Update the Blue Sheet: modes, support, Win-Results, red flags, actions, change log.
5. Draft the follow-up (for approval).

The `mh-call-debrief` skill does this from notes or a transcript.

## Common mistakes

- Giving information (a product tour) before getting information.
- Walking in without a BAC, so the call ends with "let's keep in touch".
- Treating a title as the Concept ("the CFO cares about cost") instead of asking.
- All new-information questions and no attitude questions, so you learn facts but not Wins.
- Skipping confirmation questions and acting on stale information.
- Forgetting to update the Blue Sheet, so the next call restarts from zero.
